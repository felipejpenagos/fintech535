Thesis

Options on these stocks are priced for more movement than the stocks deliver (implied volatility above realized). The wheel is a seller of options every week, so it should collect that gap. It holds the stock only part of the time and caps its upside, so it should be less volatile than buy-and-hold with a similar return: a higher Sharpe ratio. It should lag in a strong rally (calls get called away) and still lose in a crash (puts get assigned on the way down).

That is three testable claims: (1) implied > realized on the options it sold; (2) lower volatility than holding; (3) higher Sharpe. The Results tab checks each one.

2. The cash-secured put

Sell one put with strike K for premium p and set aside the cash to buy the shares:

$$\text{collateral} = 100\,K, \qquad \text{P\&L at expiry} = 100\,\big[\,p - \max(K - S_T,\,0)\,\big]$$

AAPL, first week: stock 267.27, sold the 262.50 put for 1.19: collateral $26,250, premium $119, or 0.45% of the collateral in one week. If AAPL closes Friday below 262.50, the wheel buys 100 shares at 262.50; its effective cost is K − p = 261.31.

3. Why a cash-secured put is a covered call in disguise

$$C - P = S - K e^{-rT} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \underbrace{K e^{-rT} - P}_{\text{cash + short put}} \;=\; \underbrace{S - C}_{\text{shares + short call}}$$

Put-call parity says the two phases of the wheel have the same payoff. The wheel is a covered call that sometimes holds cash instead of shares: after a call is assigned it re-enters through a put, buying back in below the current price if the stock falls. Both phases are short an option, so the whole strategy is short volatility.

4. Where the money comes from: implied vs realized

A short option, hedged for direction, earns or loses each day

$$d\Pi \;\approx\; \tfrac12\,\Gamma\,S^2\left(\sigma_{\text{implied}}^2 - \sigma_{\text{realized}}^2\right)dt$$

Γ is the option's gamma. If the stock moves less than the option price assumed (realized < implied), the seller keeps more premium than the moves cost. That gap is the variance risk premium: investors overpay for protection. The wheel does not hedge its delta, so it also carries the stock's direction (about 0.3 of a share in the put phase, about 0.7 in the call phase).

5. Choosing the strike: delta

$$\Delta_{\text{put}} = e^{-qT}\big(N(d_1) - 1\big), \qquad \Delta_{\text{call}} = e^{-qT}N(d_1), \qquad d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + (r - q + \tfrac12\sigma^2)T}{\sigma\sqrt T}$$

Each Monday the algorithm sells the out-of-the-money option whose |Δ| is closest to 0.30, with σ implied from that option's own mid. A fixed delta keeps the chance of assignment roughly constant (around 25–30% a week) whether the stock is at $40 or $340, which a fixed dollar distance would not. In this sample Apple 21%, MP Materials 31% of the options sold were assigned.

6. Capital and the collateral

Capital is the most cash the strategy ever needed: 100 × K while a put is open, the cost of the shares while holding them, net of premiums collected. Return on capital = P&L / capital. While a put is open the collateral sits in 3-month T-bills and earns their rate (Schwab flags this opportunity cost; brokers allow it); a variant without interest is in All runs.

Expired weekly options are not in any LSEG chain, so every contract is requested by its RIC, e.g. MPW072505200.U^K25: MP, W = November put (puts use M–X), day 07, 2025, strike $52.00, expired (^K25, always the call letter). Each week: the strikes from one above the Monday price down to −8% (AAPL) or −15% (MP) for puts, and up to +8% / +15% for calls; hourly bid, ask and last trade, Monday to expiry.

Fill assumption: the mid, and why

For every hourly option bar that also printed a trade, the last trade regressed on the bar's mid:

Slope ≈ 1, intercept ≈ 0 and R² > 0.99: trades print at the mid on average, so the mid is an unbiased fill. It is not a guaranteed fill, so All runs repeats the backtest paying half the spread and the full spread. MP's spreads are wide (cheap options), which is where that matters.

No look-ahead

Each Monday's strike is chosen from that morning's quotes; the fill uses a later bar; the delta uses the prior day's T-bill rate and trailing dividends; expiries come from the trading calendar (Thursday in Good Friday, Juneteenth and July 3 weeks). The two stocks were chosen by hand, which is the one hindsight choice.

Validation

validate_wheel.py re-derives the book: cash reconciles to the blotter row by row; NAV equals cash + shares − the short option's mark; shares are only 0 or 100; a put is only open while flat and a call only while holding (never naked); every option fill sits inside the quoted bid/ask of its bar on a contract whose RIC encodes that strike and expiry; assignments match the official close and happen at the strike; the attribution sums to the P&L.

Limits

  • One year, two stocks: one up path and one down path, not a distribution.
  • Assignment is modeled only at expiry; an in-the-money call can be exercised early before an AAPL ex-dividend date.
  • Fills are the quote at the end of the next hourly bar, not a guaranteed execution.
  • Delta comes from a European model on American options and is used only to rank strikes.
  • One contract: the capital follows the stock's price, so it is larger in AAPL's second half than its first.

The arithmetic of the trade

At expiry the covered call pays the stock's terminal value capped at the strike, less what the shares cost, plus the premium collected:

$$\Pi_{\text{CC}}(S_T) \;=\; \min(S_T,\,K) \;-\; S_0 \;+\; P$$

Holding the shares alone pays \(\Pi_{\text{BH}} = S_T - S_0\). Subtracting one from the other collapses the whole comparison into a single line:

$$\Pi_{\text{CC}} - \Pi_{\text{BH}} \;=\; P \;-\; \max(S_T - K,\,0)$$

Premium received, less the upside handed over. Nothing else matters.

\(S_T \le K\)The call expires. You keep the shares and the whole premium, and you beat holding the stock by exactly \(P\). \(K < S_T < K + P\)Assigned, but the premium more than covers the upside you gave away. Still ahead. \(S_T \ge K + P\)Assigned and behind. Every dollar above \(K+P\) is a dollar you would have kept by doing nothing.

So the strategy is a bet that the stock finishes below \(K + P\) — a short volatility position wearing equity clothes. It wins on flat and falling tape and on round trips; it loses on sustained trends. The most it can make is fixed the moment the call is written, while the downside is the stock's own, cushioned by \(P\):

$$\text{max profit} = K - S_0 + P \qquad\qquad \text{breakeven} = S_0 - P$$

How the rules were set

Choosing the strike

The premium on a call splits into intrinsic value, max(S−K,0), and time value. Only the time value is income. Writing a call already in the money collects the intrinsic portion but commits to selling below the current price, handing it straight back, so the strike sits at or above spot and the whole premium is time value.

Waiting through expiry

There is no roll and no buy-to-close. On the last session of the week the call either finishes below the strike and expires — shares and premium both kept, and the next week's call is written against the same shares — or it finishes above, the shares are called away at the strike, and the following Monday starts flat with a fresh purchase. Fixing the exit this way means every difference in the results traces back to the strike rule rather than to discretion later in the week.

Filling at the midpoint

Orders fill at (BID + ASK) / 2 at the timestamp of the order. A multi-leg order is quoted and competed on as one net price rather than two separate legs, so fills cluster nearer the midpoint than the two visible spreads would suggest. Where a bar carried no two-sided quote there is no fill and the week is skipped; inventing a print would manufacture returns that no one could have captured. The regression above is the test of whether the assumption holds on this particular name.

Why Reg T

Reg T is computable from the position alone, without a broker's risk engine, and it does not vary between brokers. It is also the stricter test: a book that clears Reg T clears portfolio margin, while the reverse does not follow. The short call adds nothing to the requirement here because the shares already cover delivery — a naked call, with no bound on what it could cost to buy the stock and deliver, would be charged heavily.

What this is

Every night a GitHub Action runs wheel_algo.py. It reads the prices Sanji pushes into this repo, the book Sanji writes back, and live option quotes from LSEG. It decides the next session's trade and writes it to mkt/open_orders.json in Sanji's order language. Sanji fills it, updates the book, and the next night starts from there.

Latest decision

An empty order book ([]) is a real decision: on nights when options are cheap, the algorithm sells nothing.

Why sell options

An option is insurance on the stock. Sellers usually charge more than the moves turn out to be worth: implied volatility (IV), the move priced in, tends to exceed realized volatility (RV), the move that happens. That gap is the volatility risk premium. In my HW4 wheel backtest, IV at sale beat the following week's RV in about 80% of weeks on both AAPL and MP. The wheel collects it every week without forecasting direction.

Why the filter

If the edge is "insurance is overpriced", the robot should only sell when it looks overpriced. So before each sale it compares tonight's at-the-money IV with the stock's last 20 days of RV, and sits the week out when IV ≤ RV.

What would prove it wrong

  • Live weeks where RV keeps beating the IV we sold at: the premium is not there.
  • The filter skipping the best weeks. See the Backtest tab: on MP it did, because trailing RV spikes right after a crash, just when premiums are richest.

The two volatilities

  • IV: LSEG daily bid/ask for the put and call nearest the money on the weekly expiry the order will trade. Black-Scholes is inverted on each midpoint and the two are averaged. Only the derived IV is printed or saved.
  • RV: standard deviation of the last 20 daily log returns in price_history.json, times √252.

The strike: 30 delta written as a percent

Sanji rejects delta:30 until Robin stores chain snapshots, so the script turns 30 delta into a pct token with Black-Scholes:

put:  N(d1) − 1 = −0.30  →  d1 = N⁻¹(0.70) = 0.524
call: N(d1)     =  0.30  →  d1 = N⁻¹(0.30) = −0.524
ln(S/K) = d1·σ√T − (r − q + σ²/2)·T      →   pct = (K/S − 1) × 100
σ = tonight's IV (RV if IV is missing), T = days to the target Friday / 365

Example: σ = 51%, T = 11 days gives a put at about pct:-4. Higher vol pushes the strike further out on its own.

Does the filter help? Replaying HW4

The HW4 wheel engine was rerun on the same cached LSEG data (AAPL and MP, Nov 2025 to Oct 2026, weekly 30-delta options), unchanged except for the gate. "Filter on puts" gates only the put sales; "filter on puts and calls" gates both.

What it shows

  • Options were usually rich: ATM IV beat trailing RV on about 4 Mondays in 5 for both stocks.
  • AAPL: the full filter raised Sharpe, but mostly by skipping covered calls during the rally, so the shares kept their upside. That is a bet on direction, not the volatility edge.
  • MP: the filter hurt. Trailing RV jumps right after a crash, so the gate skipped the weeks when premiums were richest.
  • Verdict: on one year and two stocks, the filter does not reliably beat the plain wheel. It stays on live as an experiment and can be switched off in one line (WHEEL_FILTER: off). A forward-looking comparison, such as IV against an RV forecast, is the next thing to test.
